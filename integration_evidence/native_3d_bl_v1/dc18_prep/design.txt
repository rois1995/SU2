1. ONE CURVED-AUTHORITY INTERFACE (2D AND 3D)
---------------------------------------------
template <int D> class CurvedReference (CNativeCurvedReference.hpp):
  Elements(), Component(element), Marker(element)
  Evaluate(element, u, v)  point at the parameters. 2D: edge and t. 3D: facet and barycentric (u, v) of corners 1 and 2.
  Normal(element, u, v)    unit normal of the element's own side; per side at creases.
  Curvature(element, u, v) -> Principal {k1, k2 (|k1| >= |k2|), d1, d2, guard}
                           the guard is the largest turn curvature 2 sin(theta/2)/L of the smooth element edges.
                           A coarse wall cannot hide a small radius from it, as in CReferenceWall.
  VertexFeature(vertex)    0 smooth, 1 crease/feature, 2 corner
  Project(p, SearchHint)   -> Foot {point, normal, distance, component, element, u, v, feature, fallback}
                           SearchHint {component, element, rings}: a window of element rings around the hint element
                           inside its component (production), or a whole component, or everything.
Shared helpers (dimension templates): EdgeCubic<D> (Bezier edge curve, D1/D2, convex-hull Bulge),
MakeEdgeCubic<D>(a, b, ta, tb, HandleRule), TangentFromNormal<D>, CircleTangent<D>/CircleCurvature<D>,
CurvatureSizeRule, CurvatureSizes, and CurvatureMetric (3x3 tensor for the metric intersection).
The 2D curve and the boundary curves of the 3D PN patches use the same edge-cubic code.

2. DIMENSION-SPECIFIC EVALUATORS
--------------------------------
3D: PNTriangleSurface (one cubic PN triangle per original facet, Vlachos 2001)
  - Edges: crease if the faces turn by more than ADAP_ANGLE, at a marker junction, or at an open border or
    non-manifold edge. Concave creases count too: this is geometry, not the BL convexity rule.
  - Smooth sectors per vertex: the incident faces connected across non-crease edges. One normal per sector, so a
    crease vertex keeps one normal per side.
    Weights: Area, Angle or Max (Max 1999, the default). The tests show why:
      * area or angle normals have O(h) error on irregular stars. They give second-order PN only, and curvature does
        not converge (3-7 % on the icosphere, 20-50 % jittered).
      * Max normals give fourth-order PN (sphere and jittered sphere: order 3.7-4.0) and exact sphere curvature.
      * On the M6, max and angle give the same LE radius within 1 %.
  - Crease and border sectors get a one-sided quadric height fit, z = d x + e y + a x^2 + b xy + c y^2. It uses two
    smooth face rings (three at corners), never crosses a crease, and is refitted in the corrected frame. It gives the
    normal and the curvature tensor. This is needed: the sector-average normal there is tilted by O(h), and that
    gives an O(1) curvature error next to every crease. Measured: the half cylinder had 42 % at the 90 deg rim. With
    the fit it has 0.4 % at n = 64, and the open cylinder converges at order 2 including its borders.
  - Mirror rule at reflective planes (planar markers = MARKER_SYM kinds): a wall sector that meets the plane nearly
    orthogonally (|n.s| < sin(ADAP_ANGLE/2)) gets n' = n - (n.s)s. This is the user's symmetry column rule. It is
    exact by symmetry: the half-cylinder side curvature error is 1e-8.
  - Edge curves belong to the edge, in canonical orientation (a < b):
      smooth edge: PN boundary curve. The tangent is the chord projected on each endpoint's sector tangent plane.
      crease edge: built from crease tangents only. At a regular crease vertex (two crease edges, smooth turn) the
                   tangent is the circumcircle tangent of (prev, v, next), which is exact on circles. At a corner
                   (degree != 2, or a turn > ADAP_ANGLE) the tangent is the chord, so the end is straight. Next to a
                   planar face, the tangent is projected into its plane, so the junction curve stays in the
                   symmetry plane.
    Handle rule: PN (|projected chord|/3, Vlachos), the 3D default, or Circle (2L/(3(1+cos a)), exact on circular
    arcs).
  - Patch: the 10-point cubic net. Its 6 edge points come from the edge curves, b111 = E + (E - V)/2. Evaluate on an
    edge or at a vertex uses the canonical edge curve, so both sides give bitwise the same points.
  - Safety: a patch whose sampled normal turns more than acos(0.2) from its facet, or whose net bulge exceeds 0.5 x
    its longest edge, is a fallback patch. Its edges become straight and its neighbours are rebuilt until nothing
    changes. Fallback is never silent; the count is in the statistics. On the M6 it is 0.
  - Curvature: Rusinkiewicz 2004 per face (II from the normal differences along the 3 edges, using the sector normals
    of the face's own side), rotated into each vertex sector frame. Weights are mixed Voronoi areas (Meyer). Crease
    and border sectors use the quadric tensor. At a point, the three corner tensors are rotated to the patch normal,
    averaged barycentrically and diagonalized in the tangent plane.
  - Certified faceting bound of a flat child triangle (vertices at parameters r0, r1, r2 of one facet): the sub-patch
    control net comes from blossoming, then the convex hull gives max |S - T| and the normal deviation. Test: never
    below the sampled value, 1.33-1.50 x it. A rigorous per-facet bulge bound is also kept for search pruning.
2D: CurvedPolyline (cubic Bezier/Hermite per edge; generalizes what CReferenceWall does, but local)
  - Chains: connected wall segments over all markers, one per closed body or open chain. Orientation is kept from the
    input (fluid on the left). Branching is rejected.
  - Features: open ends, marker junctions (option), turn > ADAP_ANGLE, and forced vertices. On the 30P30N these are
    the slat cusp (163 deg), the blunt slat, main and flap TE corners (60-113 deg) and the main-element cove corners.
  - Per-side vertex tangents: the circumcircle tangent at smooth vertices. At a feature vertex, the one-sided tangent
    of the circle through (v, next, next2) (the reflection of the neighbour's circle tangent about the chord), or the
    chord when the neighbour is also a feature.
  - Edge cubic with the Circle handle (default). An edge that is not monotone along its chord becomes straight.
  - Curvature: of the cubic, with the circumcircle guard at smooth ends (as CReferenceWall: max(spline, turn)).
  - The PolylineReference API on the curve: At(chain, arc), Parameter, Interval, Locate, Deviation (exact arc-chord
    bound: the line distance and the overshoot are cubics per piece and their extremes come from real roots, as in
    CReferenceWall::ArcChordDistance), TangentialSize, ProjectInterval (never leaves the interval).
  - Why local rather than the global spline of CReferenceWall: every quantity uses 1-2 neighbours, so a rank with a
    one-ring halo evaluates its edges exactly like the serial code (parallel-ready). Accuracy is the same: leave-one-
    out on the original RAE2822 wall gives 4.0e-6 median vs the spline's 4.4e-6, and the flat polyline's 4.0e-5.

3. CURVATURE SIZE RULE AND HOW IT ENTERS THE WALL METRIC FOR W
-------------------------------------------------------------
CurvatureSizeRule::Size(kappa) = clamp(min(c sqrt(2 R h0), 2 sqrt(eps (2R - eps)), hmax), hmin, hmax), R = 1/|kappa|.
  - c sqrt(2 R h0), c = 0.7: the TWO_PASS rule (CReferenceWall SizeRule; CBoundaryLayerRemesher.cpp:1073-1078). Its
    chord height is L^2/(8R) = c^2 h0/4 = 0.12 h0. This is consistent with the 10 % first-height tolerance:
    faceting stays below the height tolerance.
  - 2 sqrt(eps (2R - eps)) ~ sqrt(8 eps R): the chord-height rule. eps = ADAP_HAUSD for a geometric tolerance, or
    eps = h0/2 as in the existing analytic hook (CBoundaryLayerMetric.cpp:1938-1941).
  - h0 is the marker's h0 of the current event, so the rule follows the h0 ramp: h0_k = 2^(N-k) h0 refines the wall
    progressively.
  The rule returns the largest admissible edge L*. The metric unit size is L*/1.8, so the final edge gate 1.8 enforces
  |e| <= L*, as the analytic hook does (curvature eigenvalue (1.8/chord)^2).
  Directions: (L1, L2) from (|k1| or the guard, |k2|) along (d1, d2). At the LE, d1 is chordwise and k2 ~ 0, so only
  the chordwise size is constrained. This is the anisotropic surface metric the gap audit asks for (A(a)).
Entry into W (3D): through the BL wall sample, so every consumer sees the same law: W, V, the audits, and the vertex
  tensors of the W prefilter.
  - CBoundaryLayerMetric.cpp:1934-1952: the analytic-only curvature branch becomes "authority has curvature". The
    curved reference gives (k1, d1, k2, d2) at the foot, and the rule gives (1.8/L1)^2, (1.8/L2)^2.
  - WallMetric3D (CBoundaryLayerMetric.cpp:85-153) today takes one rank-one direction (cylinder) or isotropic
    (sphere). Extend it to two rank-one lower bounds M >= l1 d1 d1^T, M >= l2 d2 d2^T, applied in turn with the
    existing never-relaxing update (:131-144).
  - The sample is intersected with D1 before the fade, so it carries through the BL band like the face-size tensor.
    It is never weaker than the wall face sizes.
  Then PhaseW10 (BLOptionA10.inl:259-286) finds the LE edges long and sorts them longest first (:275), so W refines
  the M6 LE before anything else. With the curved authority (section 5), the split point is realized on the curved
  surface.
  The W prefilter estimate (CNativeBLPipeline3D.hpp:424, dc13::EstimateLength on the event vertex tensors) is built
  from the same composition, so it includes the term automatically. Check that eventTensors are recomputed after this
  change.
  Faceting gate (both dimensions), as in native 2D (CNativeEngine2D.hpp:206): split demand = max(metric length / 1.8,
  child deviation / eps_f). In 3D the child deviation is the certified ChildDeviation bound.
Entry in 2D: CBoundaryLayerMetric.cpp:1835 (ht = closest segment length) becomes ht = min(length, L*(kappa)/1.8) with
  the curved-polyline curvature at the foot. The engine's SPLIT demand from the reference deviation
  (CNativeEngine2D.hpp:206) becomes the chord rule with eps = ADAP_HAUSD on its own once the reference is curved.

4. ROBUSTNESS
-------------
  - Projection: the candidates are the hint window, ordered by the lower bound (facet distance - certified bulge) and
    stopped when the bound exceeds the best foot.
    3D per patch: Gauss-Newton with backtracking in the simplex. Near-boundary iterates snap onto the boundary. Then
    an exact 1D Newton runs along each active edge (the edge curve), with a vertex KKT rule and re-entry to the
    interior when that descends. Descent is accepted within 1e-13 relative, because the squared distance resolves a
    foot only to sqrt(eps).
    2D per edge: best of 9 samples (a cubic edge can hold two minima), then backtracked Newton.
    Exact fallback to the facet or chord foot when the curved problem is degenerate, flagged in Foot::fallback.
    Measured: 0 fallbacks on the sphere, the box and the M6. The sphere foot error is at the PN deviation; the box
    error is 1.5e-15. 2D feet equal dense sampling to 5.5e-15.
  - Watertightness: edge curves belong to edges. The raw-patch gap across every interior edge is <= 4.3e-14 on the
    M6, and 3-4e-16 on the analytic cases; on the edge itself it is bitwise.
  - Planes: planar markers keep the plane normal for their sectors and in-plane crease tangents, so they stay planar
    by construction: 6e-17 on the half cylinder. The M6 symmetry plane is as exact as its input (4.6e-12 = the input
    |y|). The optional projection onto the fitted plane (projectPlanar) is for noisy imports; that case belongs to the
    existing DC-17 affine authority (FitPlanarMarkers).
  - ADAP_ANGLE rule: the same threshold as the BL D1/option-A creases (ADAP_ANGLE; M6 60 deg). Marker junctions
    are creases by default.
    Risk: a coarse smooth wall whose facet turn exceeds ADAP_ANGLE is treated as a crease. The M6 LE turns at most
    30.9 deg < 60, but a smaller ADAP_ANGLE could cut the LE. Report: creases whose turn is close to ADAP_ANGLE.
  - Multi-marker walls: markers are attributes. Junctions are creases (option) whose shared curve is identical from
    both sides. Planar markers are detected by role (config kind), never by name.
  - Several bodies: components (bodies) are the connected facet sets (3D) or chains (2D). Production projections
    always pass the element hint (the facet or edge the wall point is bound to). The window then lies inside that
    body, so a foot never jumps across a gap.
    Tested on 2 spheres 0.05 R apart: 0 jumps in 5120 probes at 0.25 and 0.49 of the gap.
    Tested on the 30P30N (3 bodies, minimum gap 0.229): 0 hinted jumps in 2440 probes. The unhinted global nearest
    jumps only beyond half the gap (142 cases, all at 0.6), which is the correct nearest point.
    A wing with a fuselage that touches it is one component with a junction crease. Slat/flap/main in 3D are
    separate components.
  - Degenerate or unsafe patches fall back to straight edges, with counts. Construction throws on degenerate facets,
    out-of-range ids and branched 2D graphs.

5. PARALLEL-READY DESIGN
------------------------
  - Construction uses only 1-ring data: sector normals and crease tangents from the vertex star, quadric fits from
    2-3 smooth face rings, edge curves from their two endpoints, patches from their three edges. It is deterministic
    in facet order.
  - Every query is const and allocation-light. There is no global state, no cache and no pipeline globals (unlike
    dc9::*), so threads and ranks can query concurrently.
  - Distribution: like CBoundaryLayerMetric, which replicates all wall faces on every rank ("The faces of the walls
    are gathered from all ranks"), each rank builds the same reference from the replicated original facets, sorted by
    id. This is cheap: the M6 test (2 constructions on 46.9k facets, all measurements and 11k projections) takes
    39 s with 67 MB peak RSS. A halo variant (owned facets + 3 rings) gives identical values for the owned facets.
  - Nothing is mesh-dependent: the reference is a function of the immutable original geometry only (like
    PolylineReference). Restarts rebuild it bit-identically from the original facets + ADAP_ANGLE + marker roles.
    The .native_ref sidecar does not change; the JSON report gets a fingerprint (hash of the control nets).

8. RISKS AND OPEN POINTS
------------------------
 R1 The rule at the true h0 dominates the wall budget: x13.8 wall vertices on the whole wing (~292k bisection
    points), mostly chordwise. The 500k target is a metric complexity, but this is large. A user decision is needed:
      - k = 0.12 h0 chord height (TWO_PASS, consistent with the 10 % height tolerance);
      - eps = h0/2 (the existing analytic hook, x4.9);
      - eps = ADAP_HAUSD (x1.06; geometric only, LE faceting 0.1 mm = 50 true h0).
