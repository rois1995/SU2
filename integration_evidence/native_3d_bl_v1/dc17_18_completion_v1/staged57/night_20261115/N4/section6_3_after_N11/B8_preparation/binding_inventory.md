## Activation, lifetime and early-return boundaries

`ContextValue<T>` stores a reference to an `std::optional<T>`. Its constructor calls `storage.emplace(...)` at the declaration point; `Get()` returns a reference to the active value; its destructor calls `storage.reset()`. Copy and move are deleted. Consequently, each alias's lifetime is exactly the original lexical scope of its `ContextValue`; using a new persistent owner does not permit moving activation earlier, delaying reset, or changing initializer arguments. The destructor resets in reverse declaration order.

Before `EngineContext context(...)` is constructed (current source around 1599), setup-prefix `ContextValue`s are already active, along with timer session, D9 profile, wall/lane counters, restore-budget owner, atlas state, and local `private_parameters` / `private_geometry` optionals. The mapped-geometry failure branch is guarded by `mapped_high`; when it elects failure, it constructs and returns a rejected `EngineResult` directly, without calling `finish()`. This is the pre-context rejection path. Keep its exact collective guard, returned fields (`reason`, `stop`, `maximum_work_bytes`), and destruction point; do not funnel it into the later result finalizer.

After that branch, geometry resolves, then `EngineContext` binds references to the same setup, world, accepted/original inputs, geometry, composition/options, intent, bidirectional flag and surface target. Its constructor is defaulted-member construction plus references; it does not start state lifetimes or perform collectives. Context payloads are activated by later `ContextValue`s at their original declaration points. `finish()` is declared only after these values and the stage-observer callback are initialized, so its captured aliases refer to active objects on all its call paths.

After context creation, S10–S19 early failures use `return finish()` at the original branch. `finish()` performs its existing initial vote, conditional proofs/rejected-state callback, final accounting, wall census/progress output and `working` swap/move. Only then does function-scope unwinding destroy the active `ContextValue`s and owners. Do not return a status that lets an enclosing scope unwind RAII before the original `finish()` body, and do not call `finish()` twice. S15's nested status lambdas return `false` to their original caller; those callers retain responsibility for the one `finish()` call.

## RAII and sequencing constraints

- `timer_session` is emplaced before `d9_profile`, wall/lane counters, pre-context ContextValues, geometry staging and `EngineContext`; its `ReservedBytes()` remains added to `options.caller_bytes` only when it owns the session. Preserve reverse destruction and do not add timer operations or bytes.
- S10's initialization `Timer` encloses the entire agreement/build block. S11 and S13/S15 have the existing scoped timers and phase booleans/started clocks. `finish()` closes active region/layer elapsed accounting before final proof and output. Keep those owners outside extracted block scopes when their state is needed by `finish()`.
- S13/S15 local `Timer`, `FrozenField`, `FieldAccounting`, `unique_ptr` and donor objects destruct at their existing block/iteration points. S15 explicitly sets `source` cavity context immediately before importing, then clears it immediately after; preserve this order on both success and failure paths.
- All direct `world` collectives, `context.exchange.Vote/Admit`, `world.exchange`, `world.maximum`, and directory/source calls stay in the same guarded order. In particular, do not eagerly evaluate short-circuited operands or turn one conditional collective into unconditional participation.

Preserve the separate S4 pre-context rejected return exactly. Keep S15's nested `plan`, `construct`, and `publish` status results nested as now, and do not return through a helper that closes caller-owned timer/phase scopes before finalization.
